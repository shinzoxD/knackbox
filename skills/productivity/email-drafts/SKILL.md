# Email Drafts

Optimize for the recipient's 15 seconds: what is this, what do you want
from me, by when. Everything else is optional.

## Structure

1. **Subject**: specific and scannable — "Q3 budget: approval needed by
   Fri" beats "Quick question". Replies keep the existing subject.
2. **First line**: the point or the ask. Context comes *after* the ask,
   not before.
3. **Body**: only what the recipient needs to act. 3+ facts → bullets.
4. **Close**: one explicit call to action with a deadline or "no action
   needed". Never two asks in one email.

## Rules

1. Match formality to the relationship and stakes; when unknown, default
   to warm-professional (contractions yes, slang no). Mirror the thread's
   existing register when replying.
2. Length by purpose: scheduling/confirmation ≤ 50 words; requests ≤ 120;
   difficult news ≤ 200 with the news in the first two lines.
3. Cut on sight: "I hope this email finds you well", "Just circling
   back", "Sorry to bother you", apology-openers for routine asks.
4. Difficult emails (declines, pushback, apologies): state the position
   plainly, give one honest reason (not three excuses), offer what you
   *can* do. No hedging that hides the message.
5. Follow-ups: reference the date of the last touch, restate the ask in
   one line, add one new reason to reply now. Never guilt-trip.
6. Preserve all factual details from the user (names, dates, amounts)
   exactly; use [placeholders] for anything unknown rather than
   inventing.

## Edge cases

- **High-stakes or ambiguous goals** (negotiation, conflict): offer two
  labeled variants — e.g. "hold firm" vs. "soften and preserve the
  relationship" — and say what each trades off.
- **Emotional first draft from the user**: keep the legitimate point,
  strip the heat, and note what you removed.
- **Reply-all threads**: draft for the narrowest correct audience and
  say who you'd drop.
